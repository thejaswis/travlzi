SCOTLAND · A SLOW THREE-DAY ESCAPE

Inverness,
take it slow.

Set beside the River Ness, Inverness is a welcoming Highland city with a small-town feel. Wander between riverside paths, leafy islands and historic streets; enjoy the shifting Highland weather and let the day unfold at an easy pace.

BEFORE YOU GO

Getting to Inverness

Inverness Airport is the closest airport, around 10 miles from the city. Airport buses and taxis connect with the centre; check the current route and timetable for your arrival. Inverness is also linked by rail and coach with Scotland’s larger cities.

Inverness Airport (INV)

Usually around 20–30 minutes by road, depending on traffic and your transfer. Direct flight routes vary by season, so check services for your dates.

Edinburgh

Allow roughly 3.5–4 hours by train on many services, often via Perth. Check the timetable and any planned changes before travelling.

Glasgow

Allow around 3.5–4 hours by train; direct and connecting services may vary by departure. Check ScotRail for your travel date.

THE THREE-DAY PLAN

A loch-side outing, a little local history, and time by the river.

01

Loch Ness & Urquhart Castle

Start the day at an unhurried pace and make your way from Inverness towards Loch Ness. The loch is close to the city, and the journey gives you a first glimpse of the Highlands.

  • Visit Urquhart Castle, set above the water with sweeping views across Loch Ness.
  • Explore the visitor displays and imagine the stories of the people who once lived here; check current access, opening times and ticket details before setting out.
  • Pause in a nearby village for a warm scone or café stop, then enjoy the loch views before returning to Inverness.

Thinking about the Isle of Skye? Skye is a wonderful but much longer excursion from Inverness. Rather than squeezing it into this relaxed Loch Ness day, consider it as a separate full-day trip with an early start and substantial time on the road.

02

Discover Inverness on foot

Join a local walking tour or explore the compact city centre at your own pace. The streets tell stories of Highland history, while the river gives the city a peaceful thread to follow.

  • Visit Inverness Cathedral and take time to admire its architecture and riverside setting.
  • See Inverness Castle from the outside and check official visitor information for current opening arrangements and access.
  • Wander the old town, browse independent shops and stop for coffee when the weather turns drizzly.
03

Slow moments along the River Ness

Keep the last day intentionally light. Follow the riverside paths, enjoy the greenery and watch the changing light on the water.

  • Walk across the bridges and explore the peaceful Ness Islands, connected by footbridges and wooded paths.
  • Browse the Victorian Market for independent shops and small souvenirs, then wander through the old town centre.
  • Leave room for a relaxed lunch, a bakery stop or simply sitting by the river with no fixed plan.

A TASTE OF THE HIGHLANDS

Simple pleasures, local flavours

Seafood & local produce

Look for seasonal Scottish seafood, soups and dishes featuring local produce at cafés and restaurants.

Bakery treats

Make time for fresh scones, shortbread and a warm pastry from a local bakery.

Cosy café breaks

On a drizzly Highland afternoon, settle in with tea, coffee or hot chocolate and watch the town go by.

✳

Travlzi tip: let Inverness set the pace

Some shops and kitchens close earlier than visitors expect, especially outside busy periods. Check dinner times and book ahead where needed. Pack a light waterproof layer, keep an eye on local weather and leave breathing room between plans—Inverness is best enjoyed without rushing.
